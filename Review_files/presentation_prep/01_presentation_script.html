[0:15–1:10] Slide 2 — Title & Abstract

0:15 → 1:10
"The business problem is simple to state: engineers manually check CAD assemblies for missing parts — bolts, bearings, joints — and that check is slow, expertise-dependent, and error-prone. Miss one part and it can halt a production line.
We built a system that takes a partial 3D assembly, uploaded as a STEP file, and automatically flags what's missing and where — using a Graph Attention Network trained via link prediction. Alongside the GNN we built three supporting modules: a Gemini-powered agent called AIDA that explains predictions in engineering language, an Assembly Completeness Model that learns per-category component templates, and an Octree open-surface detector — adapted from my guide's own 2020 mesh-watermarking research — that highlights unmated joints directly on the 3D mesh.
Everything is pure Python — PyTorch Geometric, no proprietary CAD API."

[1:10–1:45] Slide 3 — Use Case Diagram

1:10 → 1:45
"This is the use-case view. One actor — the Engineer — uploads a STEP file, which always triggers two things: assembly-type identification, and missing-component detection. [1:45–2:50] Slide 4 — Overall Design

1:45 → 2:50
"Walking through the pipeline left to right. Stage 1 parses the STEP file with gmsh and OpenCASCADE — fragmenting solids, detecting contacts, computing bounding boxes and volume. trimesh then gives us exact surface area and SDF ray-casting for shape signal, and we infer an 8-class component type from that geometry — no hardcoded part catalogue.
Stage 2 builds a PyTorch Geometric graph: each node carries 22 features — type one-hot, log-volume, log-surface-area, bounding box, affine-invariant shape descriptors, SDF mean and variance, and hole count. Each edge carries 6 features — mate type, weight, and a 4-way joint-type one-hot: rigid, revolute, slider, cylindrical.
That graph feeds a 3-layer Graph Attention Network — the AssemblyGNN — producing 64-dim node embeddings, which a LinkPredictor MLP turns into missing-edge scores.
Running alongside — not after — the GNN are the AssemblyTemplateDB for gap analysis and the Octree module for open-surface localisation. Everything lands in a Streamlit dual-panel UI: a 6-section colour-coded analysis panel on the left, a Plotly 3D viewer with overlays on the right."
Right column: mention Dataset numbers only briefly — 300 curated STEP files down to 239 valid, ~32 nodes / 82 edges per graph average — since Slide 5/6 covers EDA in depth.

[2:50–3:30] Slide 5 — EDA

2:50 → 3:30
"Before modelling, we ran EDA across our full raw corpus — 2,271 assemblies, 16,829 body parts. A few things shaped every decision after this: only 72% of assemblies have any contacts at all — the rest can't form graph edges and get filtered immediately. Mechanical Engineering is 80% of the dataset — a severe class imbalance we had to actively correct for later. And nearly two-thirds of assemblies have hole-count data, which turned out to be one of our strongest features — a body with N holes is almost certainly a fastener attachment point."

[3:30–4:05] Slide 6 — Deep Insights

3:30 → 4:05
"Digging deeper surfaced two data-quality issues that directly explain results you'll see in a minute. First, 144 duplicated folders — 6.3% of the dataset — appear across category boundaries, which is a data-leakage risk if the same assembly ends up in both train and test. Second, 379 assemblies are 'over-dense' — one bolt touching 10 faces of a housing gets recorded as 10 separate contacts instead of one part-pair relationship, which inflates edge counts and corrupts the negative-sampling ratio. We used this analysis to drive our category-curation strategy in later runs."

[4:05–5:30] Slide 7 — Experimental Results

4:05 → 5:30
"This is 28 training runs, so I'll anchor on three. R15, on 270 graphs with 18-dim affine-invariant features and richer GAT attention heads, is our best-ever result: mean AUC 0.726, mean Average Precision 0.917 — that AP number clears our Phase 1 target of 0.82.
Then we scaled up — 1,760 graphs at R17, 995 at R18, richer 22+6-dim features — and AUC actually dropped, down to 0.483 at R18. More data with more diverse categories hurt us. So we did the opposite: narrowed to 3 mechanically-related categories, 239 curated graphs — that's R22, our "optimum run." AUC recovered to 0.626, and critically, validation AUC and test AUC landed within 0.001 of each other — the tightest generalisation gap of any run, which is why R22 is what we call out as optimum rather than just "highest number."
R28, from this morning, is our currently deployed model — 695 graphs on our most complex category, mean AUC 0.546, AP 0.712, promoted through our serving gate."

[6:45–7:45] Slide 10 — Performance Evaluation

6:45 → 7:45
"Three runs, side by side, showing our whole learning arc. R15 — best AP, 0.917, achieved with the P1 through P6 improvements: richer attention heads, affine-invariant features, hard-negative sampling. R20 — we pushed to 38 categories for diversity, and Average Precision fell from 0.835 to 0.749; our serving gate correctly refused to promote it because it was worse than the incumbent. R22 — narrowing back to 3 focused categories restored parity between validation and test AUC, and got promoted. The story across these three columns is: diversity without curation hurts; focus with curation recovers."

[7:45–8:30] Slide 11 — Model Comparison

7:45 → 8:30
"This is the feature-schema evolution — 13 dimensions at baseline, up to today's 22 node + 6 edge dimensions. The jump that mattered most was 16-dim to 18-dim at R15: replacing raw bounding-box features with affine-invariant shape descriptors — elongation, flatness, aspect ratios, sphericity — features that don't change if the same part is scaled or rotated. That's what drove mean AUC from 0.624 to 0.726. On the right, the GAT architecture itself — hidden dims moved from 512→256→64 down to a leaner 128→128→64 by R17 onward, alongside 8-4-1 attention heads and hard-negative sampling at 0.3× weight."

[8:30–9:35] Slide 12 — Challenges & Counter Actions

8:30 → 9:35
"Five real challenges, quickly. 61 of 300 STEP files failed to parse — timeouts and complex-geometry errors — so we built a pre-screening filter pipeline. Our AUC target of 0.85 is not yet met — best is 0.726 at R15 — richer features alone didn't fix it, so we're carrying category-specific model heads into Phase 2. More data hurt us — 1,760 graphs scored worse than 270 — so we now gate on quality, not volume. 38-category diversity dropped AP by nearly 9 points — assembly topology just doesn't transfer well across unrelated domains, so we narrowed to related categories. And we hit training instability — some epochs took 1,024 seconds instead of the usual 4 to 9, from MPS device contention on Apple Silicon — fixed with device-handling logic and a best-checkpoint promotion gate, which by the way has already blocked 4 regressions from reaching production across 25 runs.
The single biggest lesson: data quality beats data quantity, and validation-test parity is a better generalisation signal than raw AUC."
